Partner practice, step by step

  1. Mount the phone.Landscape, across your partner's eyes, screen facing out. A running armband around the head or a Velcro strap holds it. Turn off rotation lock. Skip partners with neck problems.
  2. Position yourself.Partner seated, you standing close in front with elbows bent. Hands on the sides of the head, clear of the jaw. Tilt the head down about 30° so the horizontal canals sit level.
  3. Calibrate.Tap Calibrate and mount the phone during the countdown. Hold the head still, then turn it slowly to the patient's right (your left) and back.
  4. Thrust.Small (10–20°), fast (150–300°/s), abrupt, then stop dead. Mix sides and pauses. At least six each way.
  5. Watch the eyes.A catch-up saccade back to your nose at the end of a thrust means that side is weak. Look for nystagmus while the patient looks at you.
  6. Test gaze and skew.Press and hold near the left or right screen edge to make the patient look there. For alternate cover, lay two fingers over one eye, then move them to the other.
  7. Commit, then check.Tap Done, record your findings, then see the head and eye velocity traces.

In 2023, two expert examiners watched 30 Canadian fourth-year otolaryngology residents do the bedside head impulse test. Most were not independent, and the weakest items were unpredictability, positioning and amplitude. Scores on reading HIT videos did not predict hands-on skill, which is why this trainer makes you produce the thrust rather than watch one.

Teaching simulation. Reflex gains, saccade latencies and nystagmus are approximations chosen to make each sign recognizable. They do not model any individual patient. Not for diagnosis.
